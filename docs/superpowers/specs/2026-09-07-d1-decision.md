# D1 — end of Phase 0 decision note

Date: 2026-09-07. Roadmap: `2026-09-07-studio-ops-roadmap-design.md`. Inputs: `docs/superpowers/research/2026-09-07-studio-list.md` (46 studios verified, 40 tabulated) and `2026-09-07-competitor-and-rails-check.md` (35 primary sources).

**Ruling from the founder (2026-09-07): no interviews, no questionnaires.** G1 (problem interviews) and G2 (workflow observation) were dropped. Everything below rests on what studios publish and what vendors document — it shows the gap exists, not how much it hurts or what anyone will pay. Paid pilots (G5) become the first and only willingness-to-pay evidence.

## What the desk research established

**G3 competitor kill rule — not triggered.** Neither Polish incumbent documents multi-resource bookings with conflict checks (Bookero: add-ons are form fields, cart items become separate bookings; Calendesk: quantity-limited resources hang off a service, not client-pickable), parent/child space blocking, partial deposits with a tracked balance, or after-session charges on the booking. Both do timed holds and P24/BLIK — table stakes. AllBooked does most of it but is cards-only, USD, $149+ for add-ons.

**G4 rails — viable.** Stripe Connect in Poland for a JDG; P24 + BLIK on Checkout with Connect, PLN, partial refunds. Consequences that bind the S2 design:
- "Booklo never holds client money" = **direct charges** with the studio as merchant of record. Stripe steers new platforms to **Accounts v2 / controller properties**, not legacy `type=express`; build on that.
- **P24 and BLIK have no manual capture and no SetupIntent.** A hold is therefore a pending booking with an expiry (what Booklo's `EXCLUDE` model already supports), never a captured authorisation. After-session charges are a **second payment link** (or card-on-file for card payers).
- P24 capability requires the studio page to show NIP, address, registration number, refund policy, ToS and privacy → S2 ships a legal footer on public pages.
- MCC 7333 (commercial photography) is restricted, rental MCCs prohibited for P24 — set the MCC deliberately at onboarding.
- Platform cost: 9 zł per active account/month + 0.25% + 1.35 zł per payout, on top of BLIK 1.6% + 1 zł / P24 1.9% + 1 zł / cards 1.5% + 1 zł.

Also everywhere in the price lists, unprompted: **duration tiers** (140 zł first hour, 120 zł from the 2nd; 1–2 h / 3–4 h / 5–8 h bands; 8-hour day rates), **night and out-of-hours surcharges** (+25 %, +27 %), **per-person surcharges** above a cap (+10 / +20 / +25 zł), **55-minute hours**, **hour bundles** (5 h / 10 h "karnety"), and priced extras by the hour, day or piece.

**Strict ICP (≥3 rooms AND combinations AND prepayment with a deadline): 4 of 40, 5 of 46** — MILK, Fly, Studio Up!, Studio Cynamon, Backstage. Seven near-misses fail exactly one criterion, almost always "no stated deadline". The roadmap's failure signal ("eligible list under ~60 nationwide → international only") would fire on the strict definition.

## Decisions

**1. Go — with the ICP re-cut around behaviour, not room count.** The evidence says the wedge is *pricing complexity + money consistency*: duration tiers, surcharges, per-person, extras, tiered cancellation and after-session charges are near-universal; three-plus rooms is not. Room count becomes a plan axis (the resource cap), not an eligibility gate.

New ICP: a studio that (a) publishes tiered or surcharged pricing, (b) sells combinations or priced extras, (c) charges after the session, and (d) takes prepayment or a deposit — with one to ten rooms. On the list that is roughly 25 studios, of which 18 run on email/Instagram today and 13 on Bookero.

**2. Geography: Poland first.** The evidence is Polish, Polish shipped in Phase 0, the incumbent to beat is Bookero at 13/40, and 18/40 are manual — the migration path is "from an inbox", not only "from Bookero". International stays a Phase 3 option (English is already there); no second price list before D2.

**3. Band: unchanged as a hypothesis, untested.** 349–499 PLN net per location. With one- and two-room studios now in scope, price sensitivity is higher than the concept assumed; G5 pilots decide whether a lower tier (~199 PLN) is needed. Do not ship a pricing page before D2.

**4. Rail: Stripe Connect, direct charges, Accounts v2 controller properties, P24 + BLIK + cards.** Hold = pending booking + expiry. After-session charges = payment link. Legal footer is part of S2.

**5. Phase 1 order revised by the counts** (the roadmap's default had after-charges at day 30):

| Order | Slice | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **S1 Pricing rules** — duration tiers, weekday/time and out-of-hours surcharges, per-person surcharge with a cap, extras per hour / day / piece | Every price list on the sheet needs at least two of these; today `priceCents` is one flat rate |
| 2 | **S2 Holds + collection** — pending booking with expiry, Connect onboarding (Accounts v2), Checkout with P24/BLIK/cards, direct charges, legal footer | 14/40 require prepayment; the manual 18 do it by proforma and hope |
| 3 | **S3 Change consequences** — tiered cancellation replacing the single window; reschedule/extend recompute | 31/40 publish a window; 6 contradict themselves — the rule the client accepted becomes the rule |
| 4 | **S7 After-session charges** — line items (overtime in 30-min blocks, extra people, cleaning, damage), balance, payment link, write-off | 36/40 — promoted from day 30 to day one |
| 5 | **S4 Daily action list** — holds expiring, balances due, changes to confirm, sessions ending soon | Meaningful only once S2/S3/S7 states exist |
| 6 | **S6 Compound resources** — whole-studio ⊃ rooms, shared equipment as exclusive units | 25/40; day one for a multi-room pilot, otherwise follows S4 |
| 7 | **S8 Migration kit** — future bookings import, pricing worksheet, parallel-run checklist | At the first pilot's onboarding |
| — | S5 Polish | Done in Phase 0 |

Hour bundles ("karnety") are parked with memberships/credits; 55-minute hours are a turnover setting that already exists.

## What this decision is *not* based on

No operator has said how often a combination gets double-booked, how much overtime or cleaning goes uncollected, how many hours a week the inbox costs, or what they would pay. The published rules prove the work exists; nothing yet proves it is painful enough to switch tools for. G5 — three studios paying from day one — is the first test of that, and the roadmap's failure table applies unchanged.
